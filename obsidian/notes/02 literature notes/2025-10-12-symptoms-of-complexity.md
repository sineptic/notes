> [!cite] A Philisophy of Software Design, 2nd Edition
> Complexity is anything related to the structure of a software system that **makes it hard to understand and modify** the system.
> 
> Complexity can take many forms:
> - it might be hard to *understand* how a piece of code works
> - it might take a lot of *effort to implement* a small improvement
> - it might *not be clear* which parts of the system must be modified to make the improvement
> - it might be *difficult to fix* one bug without introducing another

> [!cite] A Philisophy of Software Design, 2nd Edition
> You can also think of complexity in terms of *cost* and *benefit*. In a complex system, it takes a lot of work to implement even small improvements. In a simple system, larger improvements can be implemented with less effort.

> [!cite] A Philisophy of Software Design, 2nd Edition
> Sometimes an approach that requires *more lines* of code is actually *simpler*, because it *reduces cognitive load*.

> [!cite] A Philisophy of Software Design, 2nd Edition
> The third symptom of complexity is that it is *not obvious* which pieces of code must be modified to complete a task, or what information a developer must have to carry out the task successfully.
> 
> Of the three manifestations of complexity, **unknown unknowns are the worst**.
> 
> *One of the most important goals* of good design is for a system to **be obvious**.
